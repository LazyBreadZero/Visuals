02 · Where we’ve been

Three booms, two busts, one quiet pivot.

Virtual reality has been “about to go mainstream” since the 1990s. Each wave of belief crashed on the same rocks: heavy, expensive, and nothing you needed.Three hype cycles (1995, 2016, 2021) each ended in write-downs. The pattern is consistent: the technology advanced, but weight, price and purpose did not clear the adoption bar for mass consumers.

Imagination was never the problem.

Every wave failed on comfort, price or purpose. The tech worked in demos long before it worked in daily life.

Platform shifts take a decade.

The smartphone needed years between the first product and mass adoption. XR is on a similar clock, with the 2012 restart as year zero.

The modest device won first.

The glasses that sold in volume do far less than a headset. They add a camera, speakers and an AI to something people already wear.

03 · Where we are

The great inversion.

The big, expensive headsets are shrinking. The light, cheap glasses are taking off. Apple sold roughly 45,000 Vision Pros in all of 2025. EssilorLuxottica says it sold more than 7 million Meta glasses.Two IDC trackers, two zoom levels, one story: the market’s centre of gravity moved from headsets to glasses in twelve months, and the headset leader is losing share to a wave of Chinese display-glasses makers.

Two years ago, the question was “Which headset?” Today the fastest-growing category is display glasses, compact frames with a small screen, mostly from Chinese makers. The mixed-reality headsets that Apple, Meta and Samsung bet on are shrinking.

In IDC’s AR/VR tracker, XR display glasses grew 79.8% year on year to a 46.0% share, while mixed-reality headsets fell 22.3% to 49.3%. Regionally: PRC +85.8%, Japan +88.2%, US −24.1%, Western Europe −25.2%. IDC expects the total market to grow just 9.5% in 2026 and 78.6% in 2027.

Pure “VR” is now a rounding error: 2.8% of this tracker. Most modern headsets count as mixed reality because they have passthrough cameras.IDC classes most current headsets, Quest included, as mixed reality (passthrough). Pure VR fell to 2.8% share, −37.6% year on year.

05 · The form-factor frontier

Weight is the new resolution.

The most important number on a headset is not its pixels. It’s its grams. Headsets weigh about a pound. The glasses selling in millions weigh about as much as sunglasses.Weight and price now sort every product into two camps. Headsets cluster at roughly 515–650 g and $299–$3,499. Glasses sit at roughly 50–100 g. No product yet combines glasses-level weight with a wide field of view at a mass-market price.

07 · Where this is headed

A quiet 2026, then a leap.

Analysts expect a flat 2026 and a big 2027 as new glasses arrive. The long-term bet is simple: glasses win, goggles become a specialty tool.IDC’s outlook has the AR/VR tracker growing 9.5% in 2026 and 78.6% in 2027, a 38.4% annual rate through 2030. That curve is a bet on 2027 launches, not a trend already in the data.

09 · What it means for you

Back to reality.

Here’s what to do with all this, whether you’re buying a gadget or running a company.The strategic read: the platform is forming now, the winning form factor is glasses, and the reversible, cheap moves are the right ones to make first.

If you’re a person on the street

Four things to do

  1. Try before you buy. Wear Ray-Ban Meta glasses or a Quest in a shop for five minutes. You’ll learn more than from any review.
  2. Expect glasses, not goggles. The next gadget that sees and hears the world around you will look like normal glasses.
  3. Decide your rules now. Cameras on faces change what’s private. Agree with friends and family when recording is OK.
  4. Ignore the word “metaverse”. The money moved to AI glasses. Judge a product by what it does for you today.

If you run a company

Five questions for the next leadership meeting

  1. Where would a hands-free, AI-aware view of the world remove a step? Field service, shop floors, warehouses, training rooms.
  2. Are we planning for headsets or for glasses? Mixed-reality headsets are shrinking (−22.3% in Q2). Build glasses-first where the job allows.
  3. What is our policy for cameras at work? Privacy, IP and regulation come before the pilot, not after.
  4. Which platform bets are reversible? Meta, Apple and Google/Samsung will diverge. Prefer open standards and web content.
  5. What is our tripwire? Pick the signal (an Apple launch, display-glasses share, a rival’s pilot) that moves you from watching to piloting.
